# Hackathon 24h - Scoring Rubric (distilled from the handbooks)

Source: `reference/24_Hour_Hackathon_Handbook.pdf` (authoritative). Verify against the PDF if anything here seems off.

**Scale:** Likert 1-5. **Weighted score = (rating / 5) x weight.** Minimum possible per criterion is 20% of its weight.
**Evidence hierarchy (strongest first):** live working functionality > recorded tests/benchmarks/user evidence > limited prototype > architecture/workflow artefacts > slides and claims.

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Absent, incorrect, seriously deficient or unsupported |
| 2 | Partially addressed; substantial weaknesses |
| 3 | Meets basic expected standard, identifiable limitations |
| 4 | Clearly and convincingly addressed; minor limitations |
| 5 | Comprehensive, strongly evidenced, materially above expectation |

## Round 1 - Problem Interpretation and Solution Concept (15)
Understanding of problem 4 | Key requirements 3 | Solution relevance and fit 4 | Innovation/differentiation 2 | Initial feasibility in 24h 2.

## Round 2 - Architecture and Technical Strategy (20)
Solution architecture 5 | Technical feasibility 4 | Technology selection and justification 3 | Data/AI/algorithmic approach 3 | Implementation strategy and scope 3 | Risk and contingency 2.

## Mentor checkpoint (not scored)
Be ready to say: what is built, what works, what does not, the minimum viable demo, what to cut, and what evidence you will present. Outcomes: Continue / Simplify / Pivot / Refocus.

## Round 3 - Prototype and Execution Review (25)
Functional prototype 7 | Technical implementation 5 | Core requirement coverage 4 | UX/usability 3 | Testing and validation 3 | Team execution and time management 3.

## Round 4 - Grand Demonstration and Final Defence (40)
Solution effectiveness 8 | Prototype/technical achievement 10 | Innovation and differentiation 5 | Requirement fulfilment 5 | Usability 3 | Scalability/applicability 3 | Validation/evidence 3 | Pitch and communication 1 | Q&A/technical defence 2.
Recommended format (10 min): problem and approach 2 | live demo 2 | results/validation evidence 2 | scalability/applicability 1 | Q&A defence 3.

## Tie-breakers
1) Round 4 Solution Effectiveness, 2) Round 4 Technical Achievement, 3) Round 4 Requirement Fulfilment, 4) Round 3 Functional Prototype, 5) structured technical defence.

## Strategy implied by the weights
- 65% of marks are in Rounds 3-4 (working functionality and defence). Prototype/technical achievement alone is worth 10.
- Pitch is worth 1: never trade functionality for polish.
- Requirement coverage appears in R3 (4) and R4 (5): maintain a requirement-to-feature-to-evidence matrix from hour 0.
- Every claim needs evidence: tests, outputs, benchmarks, user feedback.
- Declare simulated or unfinished parts explicitly; misrepresentation is a rules breach.
